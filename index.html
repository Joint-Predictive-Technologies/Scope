Ten rules.
One corroboration layer.

Each rule is a concrete "if this, then surface that" unit. The product ships these ten on day one. Expanding the rule library is the long-term work. Rule 10 — cross-source corroboration — is what justifies the product existing. Any individual rule can be replicated. The layer that knits them together cannot.

RULE 01

STOCK Act New Filing

TRIGGER: New PTR appears on house.gov or senate.gov

Member name, ticker, buy/sell, amount band, filing date vs. transaction date with delay flag, direct link to source. Cross-referenced against your watchlist and the member's prior trades in the same ticker.

⚡ Fast path Domain A — Insider

RULE 02

Cluster Trade Detection

TRIGGER: 3+ congressional members file in the same ticker within 7 days

Ticker, full member list, aggregate direction, time-clustering chart. Cross-referenced against news events in that ticker during the window. Requires aggregation — slow path.

◎ Slow path Domain A — Insider

RULE 03

Trump Truth Social Ticker Mention

TRIGGER: New post mentions a company name, ticker, or sector keyword

Post text, identified entities, sentiment label, premarket/intraday price reaction. Cross-referenced against prior posts mentioning the same entity and historical price reaction data.

⚡ Fast path Domain B — Political

RULE 04

Senate Hearing — Public Company Witness

TRIGGER: Committee schedules hearing with public-company executives

Company, hearing topic, committee, date, witness list. Prior hearings for the same company, stock reaction history, and options IV change since announcement. Lead time is days — analysis matters more than speed here.

◎ Slow path Domain B — Political

RULE 05

Watched Senator or Rep Posts on Policy

TRIGGER: One of ~40 tracked accounts posts matching a sector-policy keyword set

Post, identified sector, prior pattern of this author influencing the sector. Think Warren on banks, Cruz on energy, Hawley on tech antitrust. Fast for the post itself; contextualized version follows 10 minutes later.

⚡ Fast path ◎ + Context Domain B — Political

RULE 06

Executive Insider Trade — Deviation Flag

TRIGGER: Form 4 exceeds threshold AND deviates from executive's historical pattern

Executive, company, transaction, deviation flag with explanation. Cross-referenced against recent material company news and upcoming earnings date. Pattern detection requires history lookup — slow path.

◎ Slow path Domain A — Insider

RULE 07

Polymarket Significant Position Move

TRIGGER: Volume or price move >X% on a politics/policy/geopolitics market

Market question, price move, volume, related public-equity tickers where mappable. Large position movements flagged separately from price movements. Cross-referenced against news during the movement window.

⚡ Fast path Domain C — Prediction Markets

RULE 08

Federal Register Sector-Impact Rule

TRIGGER: New rule proposal published in Federal Register affecting a tracked sector

LLM-generated rule summary, affected sectors, comment period dates, historical pattern of similar proposals. ETF and key ticker reactions cross-referenced with prior rules and their outcomes.

◎ Slow path Domain B — Political

RULE 09

Lobbying Disclosure Spike

TRIGGER: Company's quarterly lobbying spend (LD-2) increases >50% year-over-year

Company, spend delta, issues lobbied, recent regulatory context. Cross-referenced against pending legislation touching those issues. A company doubling its lobbying spend is rarely random.

RULE 10 — THE MARQUEE FEATURE

Cross-Source Corroboration Alert

TRIGGER: Two or more of the above rules fire on the same ticker within a 48-hour window

Combined timeline of all events. LLM-generated narrative: "why this is interesting." This is the rule that justifies the product existing. Any individual rule above can be replicated by a competitor. The value is in the corroboration layer that knits them together.

The user-customizable version of Rule 10 — where you define what "interesting" means across your own thesis — is what the eventual moat looks like.

Positioning
⌖

A scope, not a gun

Scope is a research-augmentation tool, not a signal or advice product. The shooter aims; the scope sees. No buy/sell recommendations. No "if you'd followed Pelosi" marketing. No AI predictions. The LLM is used for summarization, entity extraction, and contextualization — not prediction.

◈

Fast where speed is the point

A pure-speed product loses to professional terminals on infrastructure. A pure-context product loses to the user's own careful reading. The hybrid says: fast path for STOCK Act filings and Truth Social posts; slow path with LLM context for hearings and cross-references.

◉

Thesis-driven filtering

Older tools do keyword matching. Scope does semantic matching against a user's stated worldview. Type what you're watching for in natural language — "rate cuts in Q3, semiconductor export controls, Brazil political risk" — and the relevance engine filters accordingly. This is genuinely new in this category.
