OSINT-Graph / preview of a future release / not a feature tour

A company's legitimacy stacks up in public
before the market prices it.

OSINT-Graph is designed as a time-aware knowledge graph that connects people, companies, governments, assets, contracts, capital and places into events — and surfaces the moment those events converge on the same entity or location before the market has priced it. It is Scope's convergence thesis and honesty discipline, scaled from a single ticker to a graph.

The flagship chain What actually exists today
Read this first. Everything below describes an architecture and a design target, not a running product. Parts of the foundation are built — an entity-resolution spine, a geospatial index, a contract corpus and a patent corpus — and parts of it are blueprint with zero code. The build-status section lists which is which, item by item, with no rounding up. The design doc's own warning is that breaking its principles “turns a rich map into a confident fiction”; a preview page that overstated its build status would be exactly that failure, moved from the graph to the pitch.

01 / the flagship signal

Legitimacy-stacking: a narrative arriving in sequence

The design's sharpest idea is that people are the through-line, and companies are things people move between. A person carries provenance a company cannot — capability arrives with a hire before it shows up in results.

“The new CTO previously worked at Lockheed and published on hypersonics” is a stronger early signal than anything on the company's own filings, because it is about capability preceding output. osint-system-design.md §4

Stack those arrivals on one entity over 60–90 days and you get the pattern the whole system is pointed at. This is the chain, exactly as the founding doc writes it:

Legitimacy-stacking chain · §4, illustrated
⚠ This is an illustration of the example chain written in osint-system-design.md §4 — a hypothetical microcap, drawn to show the shape of the signal. It is not a real company, not a detection, and not live data. No end-to-end run of this chain on a real microcap has been performed.
  1. A microcap appoints a new CTO Would resolve from: SEC filings / corporate officer records — the person becomes a resolvable entity, not a name in a press release.
  2. That CTO previously worked at Lockheed Would resolve from: proxy statement · patent assignment history. Provenance from filings and registries — not from LinkedIn, which the design explicitly rejects as a feed.
  3. Three published papers on hypersonics Would resolve from: OpenAlex, with ORCID as the disambiguating anchor.
  4. A patent filed four months ago Would resolve from: USPTO — inventor and assignee both carry canonical anchors.
  5. New defense-related job postings Would resolve from: the company's own career site · structured job feeds.
  6. An SBIR award Would resolve from: SBIR.gov. ⚠ This is the link the assembly plan calls “the missing middle link in the flagship chain” — SBIR/grants ingestion has never been started.
  7. A DoD contract Would resolve from: USAspending — award ID and place of performance.

Every link in that chain is authoritative and person-resolvable, and none of it requires scraping a hostile platform. That is the point: the chain is not built from rumour or from social-media activity, it is built from filings, registries and award databases that cite themselves.

And read in order, it is a narrative arriving in sequence before the market prices it — which is precisely the gap Scope cannot see today. Scope's convergence rule catches simultaneous convergence; it does not catch longitudinal accumulation. Closing that gap is what OSINT-Graph is for. If the system can surface that chain on a microcap, the founding doc says, that is the product.

02 / the pattern library

Six shapes a pre-catalyst signal actually takes

The flagship chain is one canonical pattern. Six real case studies — six tickers from the first batch run through the case-study template, with four more checked and skipped for lack of OSINT content — each surfaced a structurally distinct shape of pre-catalyst signal. Two of them surfaced genuinely new negative-signal categories, and one is a documented limit rather than a detector. They are listed here with the cases they came from, including where each one breaks.

Repeat-buyer acceleration

MOBX

A fixed buyer places sequential orders with the same small-cap supplier, with escalating scale, tier or explicitness over time — the company's own press language naming the escalation.

Case Army 2024 → Navy qualification Aug 2025 → Navy “follow-on” Oct 2025 → Tomahawk order Mar 2026; a ~2-year single-buyer relationship.
Lead time months to ~2 years — the longest confirmed of any pattern here, counted from the first order in the chain.
Needs no new source — a count of orders per buyer–recipient pair over contract data already held.
Measured independently measured once on a separate case — a single data point, not a validated rule. The rule as written is still queued for its first test.
Where it breaks Single-buyer companies are structurally unscoreable by a growth-controlled design — there is no all-agency control series to compare against. A measured limitation, not a theoretical one.

Credential-laddering

OPTT

A company alternates contracts with credentials — certifications, qualification programs, compliance milestones. The credentials are not revenue events; they are eligibility events, announced ahead of the contracts they unlock.

Case AUVSI “Trusted Operator” May 2025 → AUVSI Trusted UMS Operator Training Provider Nov 2025 → CUI-protection compliance Jul 2026, interleaved with contract wins across the same window.
Lead time weeks to months per credential.
Needs a source type not yet in Scope's contract-event schema — no structured registry for AUVSI-style certifications was found.
Where it breaks Telling a credential that genuinely gates future contracts from one that is purely reputational requires sector knowledge. Treating all certifications as equally predictive would dilute the signal.

Adjacent activity — directionality checked

QIMC

A big player's activity appears near a small company's assets. The naive read is that the big player's move predicts the small company. The corrected read requires establishing who moved first.

Case initially misread, then corrected within the same case study: QIMC's own technical presentation (Nov 12–13, 2025) triggered a 6,700-claim staking rush by other parties; the major player's move was reported three days after the rush story broke — a lagging confirmation picked up by media, not a leading indicator.
Lead time none as a leading signal — its value is as confirmation once directionality is established.
Where it breaks — the pattern's whole point Treating adjacent major-player activity as automatically predictive is exactly the error a naive detector would make. This belongs in the system as a directionality-check gate, not as a standalone positive-signal detector.

Capability-stacking via serial M&A

ONDS

A stated multi-domain capability narrative built through repeated acquisitions, each timed to close a specific named gap in that narrative — rather than won organically with existing capability.

Case Airobotics 2023 (“sense”) → Iron Drone 2023 (“respond”) → 4M Defense / Apeiro Motion / Insight Intelligent Sensors / S.P.O. 2024 → Sentrycs Nov 2025 (“cyber / soft-kill”) → Roboteam Nov 2025 (“ground domain”). Each release names the gap it closes.
Lead time the pattern takes years to complete; each individual deal is a near-real-time public signal.
The negative signal found here A regulator can freeze or block a link in the sequence — an Israeli MoD freeze on a $100M Ondas bid, Jan 2026. That is informative in the opposite direction and is designed to be its own event type, not silently absorbed into “the deal didn't happen”.

Stealth relationship — a limit, not a detector

PRSO

A multi-year private technical relationship that produces essentially zero public signal until the day it is disclosed. By construction, close to undetectable in advance by any external monitoring.

Case the Peraso/InTACT drone-IFF relationship ran privately for “more than two years” before its March 2026 disclosure — no joint patents, no co-presentations, no contract language, nothing to graph until the announcement itself.
Lead time none, definitionally.
Why it is in the library at all Its value is epistemic, not operational. It is designed to be recorded as a catalyst shape the system should not claim to predict, so that missing one is not mistaken for a coverage gap or a bug. A system that knows which shapes are outside its reach is the point.

Patent-monetization-pedigree hiring

DVLT

Leadership hired specifically from IP-licensing and patent-monetization backgrounds rather than from the industry the company's own patents cover — a tell that the portfolio's licensing value may matter more to the thesis than the product.

Case CEO (ex-CTO, Marathon Patent Group), CIPO (ex-VP Patent Development, Marathon, an 800+ patent portfolio), and a Chief Licensing Officer hired Aug 2026 (founder of the Ocean Tomo patent-auction business, $11B+ in IP transactions) — three consecutive hires from one lineage, none from the company's stated technology domains.
Lead time each hire is a same-day public signal; the pattern only becomes visible once enough hires accumulate — ~20 months between the first and third here.
Needs executive biography resolved across employers over years — the hard person-resolution problem the architecture already names as its foundation.
Where it breaks Distinguishing “genuinely building IP-licensing capability” from “surrounding a weak product with patent-monetization credibility” demands the same skepticism the design mandates for proximity. This is a weak edge on the business model — not proof the patents are valuable.

What this library is, and what it is not

Six patterns from six cases. The taxonomy states its own status plainly: not exhaustive, not yet weighted against each other, and not yet tested against real graph data. Its standing rule is that a pattern observed once is a hypothesis, not a rule, until it clears a measurement. One of them — repeat-buyer acceleration — has been written up as a rule and has a single supporting measurement from a separate case behind it, and it is queued to be the first properly tested. The rest are descriptions of shapes seen in case studies. None of the six runs as a detector.
03 / the differentiator

Anyone can pin dots on a map

The edge is not “we have a map.” Anyone can pin dots. The edge is that a person's provenance, a patent, a hiring surge, a grant, and a contract become one story when they line up on the same entity over time — and the system says so honestly, with its confidence and its gaps visible. osint-system-design.md §0

That last clause is the whole product. Confidence, contradiction and coverage are not metadata bolted onto a graph — they are designed as mandatory fields on every single edge, which is what makes the difference between an intelligence product and a rumour mill. Five commitments carry it:

  • Selection by convergence,
    not by existence     An entity or location surfaces when independent signals stack — not when one thing happens to be present. This is what keeps the graph from lighting up on noise, and it is the same selection function Scope already uses on a single ticker.
  • Confidence and contradiction,
    first-class     A contradicting_sources field is designed to be mandatory: the graph is built to record what would make an edge wrong, not only what supports it. That is the falsification principle written into the data model rather than into a policy document.
  • Coverage and signal,
    never conflated     A map implies completeness. An empty region reads as “nothing here” when it may mean “no coverage here” — and those are opposite claims. The system is designed so absence-of-data can never render as absence-of-activity, and the doc is explicit that retrofitting this later is brutal: it has to be there from the first render.
  • Direct and inferred,
    always distinguishable     An inferred edge — a shared address suggesting a possible relationship — must never render identically to a direct one such as SEC-filed ownership. Relatedly, proximity is a weak edge, never proof: two things being near each other is a low-confidence hint that only rises with independent corroboration. Most OSINT graphs die exactly here, hallucinating relationships from adjacency.
  • Human verification travels
    with the edge     Every edge is designed to carry its human-verification status, so the graph knows — and can say — what a person has and has not checked.

Canonical identity, or nothing

The whole system is a join problem: the company on the SEC filing, the recipient on the contract, the assignee on the patent and the owner of the vessel have to be known to be the same entity across sources that spell names differently. The design rule is canonical or NULL, never a fuzzy guess — a match is exact against an anchor, or it stays a low-confidence candidate flagged for corroboration, never silently promoted to a fact.

This is not caution for its own sake. Scope's own ingestion linker was parked because fuzzy name-matching ran roughly 2:1 correct-to-wrong at scale. At graph scale that ratio is a hall of mirrors, and the design says so.

Authority over volume

A DoD contract and a scraped social-media title change are not the same confidence, and the system is designed never to flatten them into identical dots. The build sits on structured, authoritative, citable sources — SEC EDGAR, USAspending, Congress.gov, FEC and STOCK Act disclosures, USPTO, OpenAlex, SBIR/STTR, corporate registries.

And it names what it refuses. LinkedIn is explicitly rejected as a feed — scraping it is blocked, forbidden by its terms, and has been litigated. It stays a thing a human clicks to verify an edge, never a source. Live-point feeds (ships, aircraft, satellite) are sequenced last, and only as corroborating edges on entities that already exist.

05 / build status, stated plainly

What is actually built, as of 18 August 2026

Everything above this line is architecture and design intent. This is the unglamorous part: OSINT-Graph is not a live feature, is not available to users, and its flagship chain has never been run end to end. Its capabilities are tracked as buildings, and the graph connecting them as roads — a road only gets poured once both buildings it connects are real. Here is the register, unrounded.

Finished load-bearing, and genuinely done

  • Entity-resolution spineCanonical-or-NULL, CIK anchoring, exact-normalized-name matching. Proven across contracts, patents and congressional members.
  • Geospatial indexCounty / district precision, proximity guard, tier-aware convergence view. Stress-tested across four sessions and survived adversarial attack each time.
  • Contract corpus4,253 events · 122 CIK companies · 97.5% anchored · 98.5% dated.
  • Patent corpus100,790+ patents across 105+ companies — independently spot-checked, idempotent, zero known conflation bugs.
  • Person-resolution methodFor patents and OpenAlex: first+last as the default merge, with the resolver acting as a review queue only. Settled by measurement, not assumption.

Mid-construction including one honest dead end

  • Patent ↔ contract proximityTested exhaustively across geography, small-N entity, full-power entity and directional framings — and consistently null. This building is closed, not abandoned: it answered its question honestly, and the road is not to be re-poured.
  • Repeat-buyer-acceleration ruleDefined, not yet tested against real data. Depends only on contract data already loaded, which is why it is first in the queue.

Blueprint only designed, foundation not yet poured

  • Patent materiality scoringThe filter deciding which patents matter. Needs CPC/IPC tagging (scoped, unapplied) plus either LLM-assisted claim scoring or a curated watchlist. This is the bottleneck for catalyst detection.
  • Demand-signal traversalStarting from a stated need and walking the graph outward to entities positioned to fill it. Fully designed, zero code.
  • Case-study-to-rule pipelineThe mechanism is designed and has produced exactly one output. Not yet run as a formal backtest.
  • Physical-infrastructure signalsNew-facility and expertise convergence, adjacent-infrastructure flagging. Named, not designed.
  • Political / relationship proximityThe “seen with X” category. Named, not designed, and likely dependent on a news/press phase that does not exist yet.
  • People-network sourcesThe deliberate no-LinkedIn list — SEC proxies, press releases, SBIR principal investigators, corporate registries. Fully designed, zero acquisition done.
  • SBIR / grants ingestionThe next source in sequence, and the missing middle link in the flagship chain shown in section 1. Never started.

Roads which connections are real today

  • Real, connecting real buildingsContracts ↔ geography (place of performance, county/district precision) · congressional trades ↔ geography (member → district) · patents ↔ assignee companies (CIK-anchored ownership edges).
  • Still lines on a blueprintPatents ↔ catalyst detection · demand signals ↔ patents · demand signals ↔ personnel · patents ↔ personnel (inventor-as-person loading deliberately deferred — 209,000 entities against review-queue capacity, a scoped decision rather than an oversight) · case-study pipeline ↔ real backtesting.

Order what gets built next, and why

  1. Repeat-buyer-acceleration test — cheapest, uses only finished buildings, zero new construction. Validates the case-study pipeline itself.
  2. Patent materiality scoring — the actual bottleneck; the building that unlocks the most roads.
  3. MOBX backtest through the case-study pipeline — the first real test of whether patents can catch a catalyst before the fact rather than explain one after it.
  4. Demand-signal traversal, prototyped — meaningfully testable only once materiality scoring exists.
  5. SBIR / grants acquisition — adds the missing middle link in the flagship chain.
  6. People-network sources — the larger acquisition project, correctly sequenced last.

Three things this page is careful not to claim

1. The flagship chain has not been demonstrated. Its middle link — SBIR/grants — has never been ingested, and the patents ↔ personnel road is deliberately deferred. The chain in section 1 is the design target illustrated, not a result reproduced.

2. None of the six signal shapes runs as a detector. They are patterns read out of case studies, and the taxonomy states plainly that they are not yet tested against real graph data. Its own rule is that one observation is a hypothesis, not a rule, until it clears a measurement; exactly one of the six has a single supporting measurement behind it, and even that rule is queued for its first test rather than running.

3. The build plan is a map, not a commitment. It is explicitly not a promise to build all of this immediately, and it is expected to change as buildings complete and roads get poured. Dates and ordering here should be read as current intent, not as a shipping schedule.
