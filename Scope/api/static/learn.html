Scope — political & market intelligence

The filings are public.
The pattern is not.

Congressional trade disclosures. SEC filings by company executives. Federal contract awards. All free, all scattered, all published weeks apart — and almost nobody reads them together. Scope does, and tells you the moment three unrelated sources land on the same company.

Congressional disclosures, Form 4s, USASpending awards, 13F holdings and more — ingested, scored at write time, and joined by a gate that fires only when three distinct instruments converge on one ticker inside a fourteen-day window.

Any one filing
can be explained away.

A senator bought an airline stock — maybe their advisor did it. An executive sold shares — maybe they are buying a house. A small company won a contract — maybe it was routine.

Every input is a commodity, and individually each has an innocent reading that is usually correct. A rule firing on one of them is an event counter, not a signal.

What is much harder to explain away is the same company turning up in several of those records at once, filed by people with no reason to be coordinating.

That is the only thing Scope treats as a real finding — which means most of what the system does is refuse to call things findings.

The value lives in the join: independent instruments, not sharing a source or a filer, landing on one ticker inside a bounded window. That is falsifiable, and it is what the gate tests.

Two consequences run through the whole product: more of the same source is not more evidence, and a source whose ticker was guessed rather than named cannot corroborate anything. Both are enforced in code.

Disclosure lag

30–45 days

Congressional trades surface weeks after they happen. Scope reads the disclosure, and says so — it never pretends to see the trade live.

Convergence window

A tour of the product

The three refusals that make it worth trusting

Instruments, not headlines

Four different alerts can all be reading one congressional disclosure feed. Together they still count as one. Otherwise you could manufacture a convergence out of a single filing.

RULE_01, RULE_01B, RULE_02 and RULE_CLUSTER collapse to one instrument. The test is same document population, not same endpoint — RULE_06 (Form 4) and RULE_15 (8-K) share the EDGAR host and stay separate.

Noise cannot vote

Reddit chatter, prediction-market odds and attention spikes are shown to you and never allowed to complete a convergence. Crowds react to news; they are not independent of it.

RULE_07, RULE_REDDIT, RULE_OSINT and RULE_ANOMALY are excluded from the eligible set. They still emit, display and score — they simply cannot be a leg.

A guessed ticker is not evidence

Some sources describe an industry, not a company. Attaching those to a list of related stocks would invent a connection that was never in the document, so they are context only.

The basket-keyed class — RULE_08, RULE_ADSB, RULE_TELEGRAM_OSINT — derives its ticker from a keyword→symbol lookup. Excluded by shape, not by a name list. RULE_08 was live and counting until 2026-07-29; removing it took a real leg away, forward-only.

And one more, easy to miss: direction has to agree

An executive selling their own stock is a real, interesting signal. It is not evidence for the idea that the company is about to do well. Scope checks which way an insider signal points before letting it count toward a bullish convergence — and when it refuses one, it says so on the alert rather than hiding it.

Per-alert signed legs, scoped today to RULE_06 alone. The bar is a genuine open-market buy: code P, acquired, non-derivative, non-10b5-1. It fails closed — an unknown verdict does not corroborate — and a rejected leg is disclosed in tags.non_corroborating. A rule may only be signed once its attribution is repaired, which is why RULE_01B and RULE_15 are deliberately still unsigned.

The two numbers

Two scores. Never added together.

Scope asks two separate questions about every alert and refuses to merge them into one rating.

How well-supported is this? — evidence confidence. And how much of the move is probably left? — opportunity.

They pull in opposite directions on purpose. A story everyone knows can be beautifully evidenced and useless to you. A brand-new signal can be thin and still worth a look. One blended number would hide exactly that difference.

Two independent axes, computed at write time and immutable thereafter.

evidence_confidence steps on the instrument count in the gate's own units: ≥3 → 40, ≥4 → 60, ≥5 → 75, plus a source-quality term; a minimum three-instrument fire persists 46.0. opportunity_score is the freshness-and-absorption axis, clamped 0–100 before the liquidity multiplier.

Scores are forward-only: re-enriching historical alerts would recompute novelty against today's population and destroy the detection-time values that outcome calibration depends on.

A path through it

Your first ten minutes

Open the brief, not the feed

The front page is a written summary of what happened since yesterday. It is the only surface that decides what matters for you. The raw feed will still be there afterwards.

/ serves the cached deterministic morning brief and never generates on page load — a missing brief falls back to yesterday's with a notice, then the feed with a notice. /brief is a different page on a different table: the LLM-written one, with a regenerate control.

Sort the feed by score, then narrow it

It opens at the loudest severity so you are not buried. Widen it once you know what you are looking at — by company, by window, by how big the company is.

HIGH+ over 7 days by opportunity. Every filter is URL state, so a view you build is a link you can keep.

Open one ticker and read its history

Click any symbol. You get every signal that ever fired on it, a timeline, a map of which sources agree, and honest context on whether a position that size is realistic.

/ticker/{sym}: all-time history, materiality context dated per filing, liquidity at a stated participation rate, the timeline, and the corroborating-versus-context graph.

Check what is almost there

The forming page lists companies one source short of firing. These are explicitly not signals, and the page says so at the top.

/forming renders near-misses with a slot meter — a count rendered as a count, never a percentage, which the page's own tests enforce.

Ask whether any of it has worked

Before trusting any of this, go and look at the scoreboard. It is built to include the failures, because a track record that forgets its losses is worse than none.

/performance is the denominator page. /status is the operational one: which rules ran, when, what they emitted, and whether anything failed silently.

Learn one keyboard shortcut

⌘K from anywhere searches tickers, members of Congress and signal headlines. It is a search box, not a page menu — everything else lives behind MORE in the top bar.

A tool that only tells you
its strengths is not a tool.

These limits are deliberate, and several are enforced in the code rather than promised in the copy.

Design constraints, held on purpose. Most are enforced by tests; where something is a known limitation rather than a resolved one, it says so.

It does

Describe a signal, its provenance, and what that kind of signal has meant historically.

It does not

Tell you to buy or sell anything. The analyst-AI stays on the descriptive side of that line by design, from day one.

It does

Show you a single striking signal — a well-known member of Congress buying something — and let you decide what it is worth.

It does not

Hide it behind the convergence threshold. The gate decides what Scope calls corroborated; it does not gate your judgement.

It does

Publish outcomes with the losses in the denominator.

It does not

Report a win rate over "signals still visible today". Decay, deduplication and fading remove signals for reasons unrelated to whether they were right.

It does

Label forming convergences as watch items in the page's own headline.

It does not

Count them as signals, or express them as a percentage that would make a near-miss look like a probability.

It does

Show industry-level sources — lobbying, regulatory notices, geopolitical events — as context.

It does not

Let them complete a convergence when their ticker came from a keyword list rather than the document itself.

It does

State plainly when a number is a placeholder or a known limitation.

It does not

Dress one up as a measurement. The win-rate term is a fixed placeholder today, and the top evidence tier saturates at five instruments — both documented, not hidden.

And the plainest one

Scope reads disclosures, and disclosures arrive late — often a month or more after the trade they describe. It is a research instrument for finding things worth investigating. It is not market data, it is not advice, and nothing in it is a prediction.

Every congressional instrument is disclosure-lagged 30–45 days, and 13F is stale by law at roughly 45. Neither is an early-warning instrument and neither is sold as one. Scope surfaces and explains; it does not instruct.
